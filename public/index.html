What you're actually signing up to

Three things
we get right

01 — The training

Free, and worth over £500

Ten weeks with professional boxing coaches. You start at the basics — stance, guard, the jab — in a room full of people starting at exactly the same place. Sessions cater to every level, first timer or not.

02 — The matching

Nobody gets thrown to the lions

You're matched on age, weight and experience, and matches aren't made until late in the camp — sometimes the last minute. That's deliberate. Getting it right matters more than getting it early.

03 — The reason

You pick the cause

Contenders have raised over £2 million since 2009 for whatever mattered to them — Macmillan, Mind, CALM, Prostate Cancer UK, a local cause nobody's heard of. Your fight, your charity.

The camp · Saturdays 10am & 11am

The ten weeks,
in order

The order matters, so here it is. The thing everyone wants to know is when sparring starts. It's week four or five, and you're walked into it.

Weeks 01 — 03

Learn the ropes

Stance, guard, footwork and the first combinations. Every session opens with a warm-up to keep injury risk down. You'll be sore. You'll also be surprised how quickly it clicks.

Weeks 04 — 05

First sparring

Controlled, supervised, and built up gradually — head guard on, coaches in the room. This is the week people are most nervous about and the week most people say they stopped being nervous.

The turning point
Weeks 06 — 08

Fight prep

Rounds get sharper, defence gets serious, conditioning gets loud. Slipping and rolling, heavy bag, ring craft. Your matchup gets considered — not rushed.

Weeks 09 — 10

Fight week

Weigh-in, matchup confirmed, walkout sorted, tickets in hands. Then the doors open, your name goes out over the PA, and everyone you know starts shouting.

The bit you're really wondering about

But…

Everyone arrives with the same six worries. Straight answers to all of them.

I've never thrown a punch in my life

Neither had nine out of ten people in the room. Camp starts at the very beginning — how to stand, how to hold your hands, how to throw a jab. Nobody is going to hand you gloves and point at a ring. The whole programme is built for people whose only fight experience is a group chat.

I'm not fit enough to start

Nobody is on week one. That's what the ten weeks are for. Sessions cater to all levels and you build from the first warm-up. Contenders come in every shape, size and age, and the ones who improve most are usually the ones who arrived least confident.

I don't want to get hurt

Safety runs the camp, not the other way round. You're matched on age, weight and experience, and matches deliberately aren't confirmed until late — sometimes the last minute — because rushing a matchup is how people get hurt. Sparring doesn't start until week four or five and it's supervised throughout. On the night you're in a head guard, 16oz gloves and a gum shield, with medical staff present.

What's it going to cost me?

No joining fee. The ten-week camp is worth over £500 and it's free. All we ask is that you sell your allocation of tickets for fight night — those sales cover the production of the show and the training camp. Get your friends, family and the office in and it looks after itself. Midweek sessions on Tuesday and Thursday are paid extras if you want more mat time.

I can't make every single session

You can train away from us in between. But you need to be in front of the coaches at least every other Saturday — otherwise there's no honest way to judge your level, and no honest way to match you safely. That's the one rule we don't bend.

I want the training but not the fight

Then take the training. Open sessions run Tuesday 7pm and Thursday 7.30pm and you're welcome in them without ever putting your name on a card. Plenty of people start there, watch a show, and sign up for the next camp anyway.
